Part 01 // The Problem

Why cold outbound is broken

The average cold outreach reply rate is 3%.

That means for every 100 messages you send, 97 people ignore you. Not because your copy is bad. Because you're reaching out to people who have zero reason to care about you right now.

Traditional Outbound

Build a list based on job titles and company size. Write a "personalized" message with {{first_name}} and {{company}}. Blast 500 people. Hope someone replies.

The fundamental flaw: there's no timing.

You're contacting a VP Marketing who's been in their role for 3 years, has their vendors locked in, and gets 47 cold messages a week. Why would they reply?

Now imagine this instead:

That same VP Marketing just changed jobs 2 weeks ago. They're reassessing every vendor relationship. They just commented on a LinkedIn post about exactly the problem you solve. And your message references that specific post.

Same person. Different timing. Completely different conversation.
That's signal-based outbound. Claude is the brain. Sortlist Signals is what makes it possible.

The 3-step message sequence

Signal-based outreach is different. The prospect already showed intent. You don't need to create awareness. You need to convert it. Shorter, faster, more relevant.

DayChannelAction

3 touches // 7 days // 2 channels

How Claude writes each message

Claude doesn't use templates. For each prospect, it reads their LinkedIn profile, the signal that triggered outreach, their company's website, and your value proposition. Then it writes a message from scratch.

Example: Job Change Signal

Sarah just became VP Marketing at a 200-person SaaS company 2 weeks ago.

Subject: Congrats on the VP Marketing role at [Company]

Sarah,

Saw you just moved to [Company]. Congrats. The first 90 days in a new role are when most marketing leaders reassess their agency and vendor stack.

We help [type of companies] [specific outcome]. Worked with [similar company] on [specific result].

Worth a 15-min call this week to see if it's relevant?

Example: Competitor Engagement Signal

Marc commented on a competitor's post about "outbound best practices."

Subject: Re: outbound best practices

Marc,

Noticed you engaged with [competitor]'s recent post on outbound. If you're actively thinking about this, we took a different approach with [similar company]: [specific metric].

Happy to share what worked. 15 min?

Example: Topic Conversation Signal

Lisa posted: "Anyone have a good recommendation for a B2B lead gen agency?"

Subject: Re: your LinkedIn post on lead gen

Lisa,

Saw your post looking for a lead gen partner. We've been doing this for [X years]. Recently helped [company] go from [X] to [Y].

No pitch, just happy to share what we've seen work. Open to a quick call?

What Claude reads before writing: profile, signal context, engagement history, original post

Message rules

  1. 01 Reference the signal. Always mention what they did.
  2. 02 Keep it short. 4-6 sentences max.
  3. 03 One proof point. One client, one result, one number.
  4. 04 One CTA. "15 min call this week?" Period.
  5. 05 No buzzwords. Factual. Specific. Short.
